# PrintMarshal — brand & identity prompt

Paste everything below into a Claude design session (logo / brand generator).

---

Create the complete visual identity for **PrintMarshal (PM)** — a logo, a mark/icon,
a wordmark, an app icon / favicon set, and a brand color system. Today the product
only has a placeholder: a plain square with "PM" in it. Replace it with a real
identity that, at a glance, says this is a **tough, secure, professional command
console for managing a fleet of network printers.**

## Anchor line

> PrintMarshal is the command-and-security console for your printer fleet. Design a
> mark with the **authority of a badge** and the **precision of an ops tool.**

## What the product is (so the mark can hint at it)

A self-hosted platform that discovers, monitors, configures, **secures**, and updates
a whole fleet of network printers across their lifecycle. It manages *devices*, not
print jobs. It is vendor-agnostic, so the identity must be fully original — it must
**not** imitate or reference any real printer manufacturer, software company, logo, or
color scheme. Everything here is generic and invented.

## Brand personality

Lead with these adjectives, in this spirit:

- **Tough** — solid, stamped, built like equipment that won't fail.
- **Secure** — guarded, authoritative, trustworthy; it protects the fleet.
- **Professional** — enterprise/operations-grade, precise, restrained.
- Calm under pressure. Dependable. Sharp.

Not: playful, consumer, cute, trendy, soft, decorative. No mascots, no stock
gear/cloud clichés, no gradients-as-decoration.

## Lean into the name

"**Marshal**" carries: order, command, authority, protection, rank, and above all the
**marshal's star badge**. "**Print**" is the domain: a fleet of devices, pages, trays,
a network of nodes. The best mark fuses **both signals** — authority/security *and*
fleet/devices — so a viewer reads "this commands and protects a fleet."

Motif vocabulary to draw from: a **badge or shield**, a **star** (marshal), **chevrons
or rank bars**, a **constellation/grid of nodes** (the fleet under one command), a
**stack of lines** (pages/devices), a **crosshair or reticle** (precision/targeting).

## Logo concept directions (explore 3–4, then recommend one)

- **A — Badge/shield + fleet.** A modern, geometric shield or marshal-star badge
  containing an abstract fleet element: a small constellation/grid of connected nodes,
  or a clean stack of lines. Authority + security + "many devices, one command."
- **B — Stamped monogram.** A strong, geometric **M** (or a tight PM ligature) set
  into a shield/badge — solid, stamped, confident; feels like an issued credential.
- **C — Command chevron.** A bold chevron (rank / forward / command) fused with a
  single node or dot (a device on the network) — motion and control.
- **D — Fleet under guard.** A central shield/anchor with aligned or orbiting nodes
  around it — central control over a managed fleet.

Rules for the mark: **geometric, bold, and legible at 16px.** Prefer an abstract hint
(a page corner, a tray line, a node grid, a star point) over any literally-drawn
printer. It must work as a flat silhouette.

## Wordmark

Set "**PrintMarshal**" in a strong, slightly industrial/technical sans — medium-to-bold
weight, confident, tight-but-legible tracking. Explore:

- A subtle **two-tone split** ("Print" in a neutral, "Marshal" in the brand color), or
  the mark accenting/replacing a letter (e.g. a star as a counter or dot).
- A **compact "PM" lockup** for small spaces and the app tile — far better than the
  current square: treat it as a stamped badge, not a text box.
- A **one-color (mono)** version that still reads.

## App icon / favicon

A bold, simple version of the mark that reads at **16px and 32px**, as a desktop app
icon, and as a browser favicon. Likely the badge/mark on a solid or subtly-dimensional
**dark graphite/gunmetal** ground with the accent color carrying the highlight. Must be
recognizable purely as a silhouette and distinct in a crowded dock or tab strip.

## Color system — "Steel & Signal" (invented, fully generic)

Use this palette as the brand identity. It is tuned to tough / secure / professional:
cool steel darks, a confident electric-steel primary, and a brass accent that nods to
the marshal badge. Provide tints and shades for each, and ensure the logo works on
light, on dark, in one color, and reversed.

| Role | Name | Hex |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ink / darkest | Graphite | `#14171E` |
| Steel surface | Gunmetal | `#2A313D` |
| Mid neutral | Slate | `#5B6676` |
| Light surface | Mist | `#E8ECF2` |
| **Primary brand** | **Cobalt Steel** | `#2E54E6` |
| Primary depth | Deep Cobalt | `#1E3AA8` |
| **Accent (badge)** | **Brass** | `#D7A442` |
| Badge highlight | Brass Glow | `#EFC065` |

- **Cobalt Steel** is the main brand color — trust, security, precision.
- **Brass** is the identity/badge accent (the marshal star's glint). Use it in the
  logo and sparingly; it is a *brand* color, **not** a UI "warning" color.
- Darks (Graphite / Gunmetal) give the tough, equipment-grade base.
- Optionally explore a cooler **"security-ops" variant** that swaps Brass for an
  **Electric Teal `#16C7C0`** accent, for a more tactical, modern-secure feel — present
  both so we can choose.

Keep the brand palette separate from the app's **operational status colors** (good
`#2FBF8F`, warning `#F2A93B`, critical `#E5484D`, info `#3B82F6`, offline `#6B7280`),
which live in the product UI and should not be confused with the identity colors.

## Typography (brand)

Pair a **display/wordmark face** — a bold geometric or grotesque sans with an
industrial, stamped feel — with a **clean, neutral UI/body sans** for everything else.
Suggest specific directions for each.

## Deliverables

- Primary **horizontal lockup** (mark + wordmark) and a **stacked lockup**.
- The **mark/icon alone**, and the **compact "PM"** badge lockup.
- **App icon** (multiple sizes down to 16px) and **favicon**.
- **On-light, on-dark, one-color, and reversed** versions of each.
- The **color tokens** (hex, with tints/shades) and the type pairing.
- **Clear-space and minimum-size** rules.
- A short rationale for the chosen direction, and 2–3 explorations before the pick.
- Deliver the mark as clean, geometric vector-style artwork.

## Constraints

- **Fully original and generic** — imitate no real company's logo, mark, or colors;
  use no real printer-vendor cues.
- Must read at tiny sizes and as a flat silhouette.
- Must feel **tough, secure, and professional** — never cute or consumer.
- Device/fleet-and-security themed — not print-job or paperwork themed.
