# Logo mark concepts — 30 October 2026 (draft, for choosing)

Seven ideas for a mark to replace the Guard Chevron shield. Brief: tech and printer security, no
shield, star, badge or police iconography, no padlock as the whole idea; the Steel & Signal palette
stays (Graphite, Mist, Cobalt, Brass). These are descriptions plus rough geometry on the sketch sheet
`logo-sketches-2026-10-30.svg`; nothing here is final. Pick two or three to render properly.

Each concept is judged on the same five things: what it says, how it reduces to a 16 px favicon and a
rounded app icon, its one-colour version, how it sits beside the new wordmark (lowercase mono
"print" + "Marshal"), and whether it looks like something that already exists.

## 1. Page Check

**Says:** a printed page that has been inspected and signed off. The product's core promise, evidence
before trust.
**Geometry:** a page outline (3:4) with the top-right corner folded; the fold line continues into a
short check stroke that breaks the page's right edge. Two strokes total, uniform weight.
**Small sizes:** the folded page survives at 16 px because it is a known silhouette; the check reads
as a notch. App icon: page on Cobalt, check in Brass.
**Mono:** one colour, open strokes; works knocked out of Graphite.
**With the wordmark:** sits left of "print", page height equal to the cap height of "Marshal".
**Risk:** document and task-manager apps use page-plus-tick (Google Tasks, several PDF tools).
Distinctive only if the fold-into-check joint is done well.

## 2. Nozzle Grid

**Says:** the print head's nozzle array, and a scan grid; a fleet is many small identical units.
**Geometry:** 3×3 dots on a square grid; the centre dot is a ring (the inspected device) in Brass,
the rest filled Cobalt. Variation: the bottom-right dot missing, meaning one device not reporting.
**Small sizes:** dots are the most favicon-proof form there is; at 16 px use 2×2 or 3×3 with 2 px dots.
**Mono:** all dots one colour, centre as a ring.
**With the wordmark:** compact square, aligns to the x-height of "print".
**Risk:** generic; dot grids are everywhere (app launchers, Microsoft's waffle). Carried by the ring and
the colour story, not the shape.

## 3. Paper Path

**Says:** the path a sheet takes through a printer, drawn as one line; what the product watches end to
end.
**Geometry:** an S-curve of uniform stroke that enters bottom-left, loops, and exits top-right into a
small terminal dot. The dot is the device node. Stroke ends are square (printer, not calligraphy).
**Small sizes:** an S with a dot reads at 16 px if the stroke is at least 2 px; app icon: path in
Mist on Graphite, dot in Brass.
**Mono:** inherently one colour.
**With the wordmark:** the curve's exit dot aligns with the top of "print"; the mark can also sit
inline as the "i" dot replacement if you want a tighter lockup (optional, risky).
**Risk:** could read as a generic route or river icon; the square stroke ends and the terminal dot
need to carry the printer meaning.

## 4. Lens on Page

**Says:** inspection; a page examined under a lens, security assessment of a document device.
**Geometry:** a page outline with a circle overlapping its bottom-right third; inside the circle the
page's lines are enlarged (two thicker rules). No handle, so it is not a magnifying glass cliché but a
lens.
**Small sizes:** at 16 px drop the inner rules and keep page plus circle; app icon: Graphite page,
Cobalt lens, Brass rules.
**Mono:** page and circle outlines, rules filled.
**With the wordmark:** circle bottom aligns to the baseline.
**Risk:** "search" and "preview" icons use page-plus-circle; the inner enlarged rules are what make it
"inspect" rather than "find".

## 5. Fleet Formation

**Says:** many printers managed as one fleet; the front page is the one being verified.
**Geometry:** three page outlines stepped diagonally (back to front), the front page filled Cobalt
with a Brass notch cut from its top-right corner, the two behind in outline. Equal offsets, no
perspective.
**Small sizes:** at 16 px reduce to two pages; the fill keeps it readable. App icon: formation on
Graphite.
**Mono:** front page filled, others outlined, same colour.
**With the wordmark:** the stepped stack gives a natural leading edge toward "print".
**Risk:** stacked documents are a common "files" icon; the fill-and-notch on the front page is the
differentiator.

## 6. Marshal Flag

**Says:** the race or parade marshal, the person with the flag who directs the field: authority
through signal, not force. Ties to the "Signal" half of the palette name.
**Geometry:** a vertical pole stroke with a right-pointing flag of two horizontal bars (halftone
bars, like printed lines), the lower bar shorter; the flag is Brass, the pole Graphite or Mist.
**Small sizes:** a flag is a strong 16 px silhouette; app icon: Brass flag on Graphite.
**Mono:** pole and two bars, one colour.
**With the wordmark:** pole height equals the ascender height of "print"; the flag points at the
word.
**Risk:** flags carry national and warning connotations if the shape is too pennant-like; keep the bars
rectangular and the pole plain, and it reads as a signal flag, not a nation or a checkered finish.

## 7. Printed M

**Says:** Marshal's initial, built the way a printer builds everything: in horizontal lines. The one
Brass line is the signal, the finding in the evidence.
**Geometry:** a capital M formed by five horizontal bars of equal height and gap, each bar clipped
to the M's outline (so the bars are of different lengths and splits); one bar, the second from the
top, in Brass, the rest Cobalt. No outline strokes at all.
**Small sizes:** at 16 px use three bars; the M silhouette holds. App icon: bars on Graphite.
**Mono:** all bars one colour; the signal bar can become a gap instead.
**With the wordmark:** doubles as a standalone mark for Marshal without the "print" word,
which the other six do not.
**Risk:** scanline letterforms exist (several media and broadcast logos); the clipped-bar M is common
enough that the exact proportions matter. Trademark search before committing, as with any monogram.

## Where I would start

- **Page Check (1)** and **Printed M (7)** are the two strongest for this product: one says what
  the product does, the other gives Marshal a mark of its own.
- **Marshal Flag (6)** is the most distinctive and the most on-name, and the only one that can carry
  "Marshal" without an initial; render it if the flag reads as signal rather than nation at 16 px.
- Nozzle Grid, Paper Path, Lens on Page and Fleet Formation are sound but closer to existing icon
  vocabulary; keep them as alternates.
