# Logo mark concepts, round 2 — 30 October 2026 (draft, for choosing)

Round 1 (`logo-concepts-2026-10-30.md`) offered abstract marks and none landed. The new brief is
literal: the mark should show a printer or MFP, so that it says what the product does rather than
being an insignia. A shield is allowed back as a frame or a silhouette, not as the whole idea. The
Steel & Signal palette stays: Cobalt line work, Brass for the thing being checked, Graphite for mono.
Sketches are on `logo-sketches-round2-2026-10-30.svg` (rendered as `.png`), each at 160 px on light,
96 px on Graphite, 32 px, 16 px and one-ink mono.

The printer itself is the same drawing in every concept: a front-view body with rounded corners, a
sheet rising from the top slot, a small status dot on the panel. That keeps the family consistent if
the mark changes later and lets the app's device icons reuse it.

## 1. Shielded MFP
Shield outline framing a small printer. Protection first, printer second. Honest about what it is,
but the printer is the part that dies at 16 px, where it becomes a shield with a smudge. Works at
app-icon size only.

## 2. Verified Page
A printer whose output sheet carries a brass check: the device was inspected and signed off. The most
literal of the six and the clearest at 16 px, where it still reads as a printer. Risk: close to the
generic "printer" glyph in icon sets; the check and the brass are what make it ours.

## 3. Scan Frame
The printer inside four viewfinder brackets: discovery and inspection. Reads as a security scan
without any badge iconography and survives 16 px. Risk: QR-code and camera-focus apps use the
brackets; the printer inside is the distinguishing part.

## 4. Pulse Sheet
The page coming out of the printer is a telemetry trace. Says monitoring and health more than
security. Good app icon, but the pulse turns to noise at 16 px.

## 5. Fleet Stack
Three printers receding, the front one checked. Says a fleet, not one device. The ghosts drop out at
16 px, where it becomes concept 2, which is a feature: one drawing scales from favicon to hero.

## 6. Shield Printer
One silhouette: a printer body that tapers into a shield, with the sheet and check on top. Printer and
protection in a single shape, and the closest in spirit to the shield the user asked about. Risk: at
small sizes the sheet-plus-body reads as a padlock or a shirt pocket; the tapered body needs more
work before it is unmistakably a printer.

## Where I would start
Render 2 and 6 properly, with 5 as the hero variant of 2. Concept 2 is the safe, legible mark; 6 is
the one with a shield in it that still shows a printer; 5 gives the fleet story at large sizes without
a separate mark. Concepts 1 and 4 can be dropped; 3 is a fallback if 6 cannot be made to read.
