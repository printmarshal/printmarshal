# App icon concepts, round 6 — 30 October 2026 (draft, for choosing)

Brief: take O1 from round 5 (filled MFP, corner letter tile) toward the Microsoft Fluent / Office
depth, while finding a way for it not to read as an Office icon now that a letter sits in the tile.
W2 from round 5 (the word with a cursor, Graphite) is kept unchanged. Sketches on
`logo-sketches-round6-2026-10-30.svg` (rendered as `.png`), each at 160 px, 96 px, 48 px and 24 px.

- **A. Shaded and shadowed.** Each block shades white to Mist, the machine and the tile cast soft
  shadows, and a highlight runs across the tile. The closest to Office, which is the worry.
- **B. Pressed blocks.** Flat white blocks with a darker band along each bottom edge, so the drawers
  read as solid parts. Depth without gradients; survives small sizes.
- **C. Extruded.** Every block gets a top face and a right face, a quarter turn toward isometric. The
  most physical read of the set and the least like Office.
- **D. Layered.** A translucent second MFP behind the main one: Office's stacked pages, but here it
  says a fleet. The ghost drops out from 48 px down.
- **E. Graphite tile, mono "p".** A's depth, but the tile is Graphite with the lowercase mono "p" from
  the wordmark and W2. Our letterform and our colour: the clearest step away from Office.
- **F. Shield tile.** The corner tile becomes a Graphite shield with the mono "p" inside: the Office
  structure, but the badge is a shield.

## Where I would start
The Office likeness comes from three things together: a capital letter, a square tile in the same hue
as the background, and the Fluent shading. Changing any two of them is enough. E keeps the shading and
changes the letter and the tile colour; C keeps the tile and changes the rendering. Either is safe; A
on its own is not. F is the strongest if the shield is to stay part of the identity.
