# App icon concepts, round 3 — 30 October 2026 (draft, for choosing)

Brief: an Apple-style app icon with a real multifunction printer outlined inside it, not a generic
printer glyph, and a shield either outlined inside the icon or embossed on the machine. Sketches are
on `logo-sketches-round3-2026-10-30.svg` (rendered as `.png`), each at 160 px, 96 px, 32 px and 16 px.

## The MFP drawing
One drawing is shared by every variant: a document feeder on top, the scanner lid, a control panel
standing out on the right, the output slot, a front door, three paper drawers with handles, and
casters. From 32 px down a reduced drawing is used (no feeder, two drawers) so it stays a machine
rather than a smudge. Uniform stroke, rounded joins, so it sits with the lowercase mono "print" in
the wordmark.

## Variants
- **A. Embossed shield, Cobalt.** White MFP on the Cobalt icon; a small brass shield with a check on the
  front door, where a vendor badge would be. The MFP is the first read. Small sizes drop the shield.
- **B. Large shield on the body.** The shield embossed across the drawers, lines showing through, check
  inside. Security is the first read, the MFP the second. The shield survives to 32 px.
- **C. Shield holds the MFP.** A big shield outline inside the icon with the MFP inside it. Clearest
  security read, but the MFP is small and at 16 px only the shield survives.
- **D. Light icon, solid Cobalt shield.** Graphite MFP on a Mist icon with a solid Cobalt badge. Reads as
  a macOS utility and suits the light site sections.
- **E. Graphite icon, brass shield.** Mist MFP on Graphite with a solid brass badge. Matches the console's
  dark surface and the dark site sections.
- **F. The glyph alone.** One-ink MFP with the shield badge, for favicon, tab bar, docs and the console
  sidebar.

## Where I would start
A and E are the same mark on two backgrounds, and D is its light twin: choose the icon background
later, the mark is settled. B is the choice if the shield must be seen at a glance in a dock. C is the
weakest because the MFP, the part that says what the product does, becomes an afterthought.
